Bash `cmd_dep_cycle` aborted its DFS on the first cycle and left nodes gray, so it both reported non-cycles and missed real ones. `src/core/dep-graph.ts` had the correct algorithm since T2, but nothing pinned the fixed behavior at the BDD layer.

- strengthened the a->b,b->c,c->b scenario from `not contain "Cycle 2:"` to exactly-one-cycle plus its member set, and gave it a SECOND in-pointer
- added a three-way overlapping-cycle scenario asserting all 3 are found (the half old bash MISSED)
- `parse_reported_cycles()` step helper yields number + member ids; the count step pins headings as 1..N and rejects an expected count of 0, which empty output would have satisfied
- new unit test for the `seen` dedup guard in `CycleFinder.record` (`deps: [a, a]`, a live input class from hand-edited frontmatter) — deleting the guard had left all 294 unit tests and every scenario green
- reordered two pre-existing unit fixtures that were vacuous under the mutation

Review caught that the first version of the new scenarios was non-vacuous only by ACCIDENT of slug enumeration order — recreated with the tickets enumerating differently, both passed against the buggy behavior. Fixed structurally rather than with a warning comment: two mutually-unreachable in-pointers mean one DFS root can consume at most one, so a bogus cycle appears in every order. Proved empirically too — `order_check.py` rebuilds both shapes under all 24 filename permutations each: clean 0/48 failures, bash-abort mutant 48/48.

Mutation matrix, each producing a failure: abort-on-first-cycle, dedup guard deleted, renderer always printing `Cycle 1:`.

215 scenarios, 295 unit tests, `make parity` green (whitelisted bash bogus-cycle count unchanged at 19 — buggy bash stays on the other side of the diff until T6).
